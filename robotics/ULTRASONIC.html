What Is an Ultrasonic Sensor?

An ultrasonic sensor is an electronic device that measures the distance of a target object by emitting ultrasonic sound waves and converts the reflected sound into an electrical signal.

1. Fundamental Concepts

Pins in the Ultrasonic Sensor

VCC supplies power to the HC-SR04. Connect it to the 5V output of your Arduino.
Trig (Trigger) triggers the ultrasonic pulses. Setting it HIGH for 10 µs starts an ultrasonic burst.
Echo goes HIGH when the burst is sent and stays HIGH until the echo is received, then goes LOW. The time it stays HIGH gives the distance.
GND is the ground pin. Connect it to the ground of the Arduino.

1. Fundamental Concepts

pulseIn(pin, VALUE)

Reads a pulse (either HIGH or LOW) on a pin and returns how long it lasted, in microseconds. If VALUE is HIGH:

1. pulseIn() is called.
2. pulseIn() waits while the pin is LOW.
3. The pin goes from 0 to 1 (HIGH): pulseIn() starts timing.
4. The pin goes back to 0 (LOW): pulseIn() stops timing and returns the pulse duration.

1. Fundamental Concepts

From Echo Time to Distance

Speed of sound: v = 340 m/s = 0.034 cm/µs
One-way time: t = s ÷ v = 10 ÷ 0.034 ≈ 294 µs for 10 cm
Distance: s = t × 0.034 ÷ 2, where t is the echo time from pulseIn()
Why ÷ 2? The echo goes to the object and back, so the echo time is twice the one-way time.
